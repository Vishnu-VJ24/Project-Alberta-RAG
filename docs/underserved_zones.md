# Under-Served Tourism Zones — Bow and Red Deer River Corridors

Date: 2026-07-10

## Purpose

This document identifies the five most under-served tourism zones in the Bow and Red Deer River corridor project area. An under-served zone is defined as a geographic area that has demonstrable tourism assets — scenic, cultural, heritage, or recreational — but lacks the access infrastructure, accommodation, visitor services, or marketing connectivity needed to convert those assets into visitor activity and economic benefit for the local community.

The findings are drawn from the asset gap analysis and the corridor geography table. They inform the transportation improvement plan, hub recommendation refinements, and the marketing and dispersal strategy.

---

## Definition of Under-Served

A zone qualifies as under-served if it meets at least two of the following four criteria:

1. **Asset-access mismatch** — Tourism assets exist but visitors cannot easily reach them without a private vehicle, local knowledge, or significant detour from main routes.
2. **Spend concentration bypass** — The zone is near a major anchor asset but captures minimal incremental visitor spend because visitors do not stop, stay, or explore beyond the anchor.
3. **Accommodation floor** — The zone cannot convert day visitors to overnight visitors due to inadequate accommodation supply.
4. **Marketing invisibility** — The zone's assets are not included in a consumer-facing TDZ itinerary, booking platform, or corridor marketing campaign.

## Zone 1 — Abraham Lake and Nordegg Backcountry (David Thompson Corridor)

**TDZ:** David Thompson
**Corridor:** West-Central (Zone 3)
**Gap rating:** Critical

### Why It Is Under-Served

Abraham Lake is one of the most photographed landscapes in Alberta — the frozen methane bubble formations in late January attract international photographers and social media visitors from across Canada and the US. Nordegg is a remarkably intact heritage coal mining town. Together they form a world-class scenic and cultural asset pair. Yet:

- Both are at the end of Hwy 11 with no transit from Red Deer or Rocky Mountain House.
- Abraham Lake has no staffed visitor facility, no washrooms, and no food service despite very high peak-day visitor loads.
- Nordegg has no commercial accommodation beyond a basic campground.
- The David Thompson Hwy scenic corridor between Rocky Mountain House and the Banff boundary is not packaged as a bookable touring route.

### Gap Criteria Met

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Asset-access mismatch | Yes | No transit, sole access via Hwy 11, no last-mile from Nordegg to Abraham Lake |
| Spend concentration bypass | Yes | Visitors drive through without stopping because there is nowhere to stop |
| Accommodation floor | Yes | No commercial accommodation near Abraham Lake or Nordegg |
| Marketing invisibility | Partial | David Thompson TDZ exists but corridor is not packaged as a bookable product |

### Recommended Interventions

- Develop a basic visitor node at Abraham Lake (washrooms, interpretive panels, small food and fuel facility)
- Create a seasonal shuttle from Rocky Mountain House to Abraham Lake and Nordegg
- Package the David Thompson Hwy as a bookable 2-day circuit: Rocky Mountain House NHS → Crimson Lake → Nordegg → Abraham Lake → return
- Identify and incentivize cabin or glamping accommodation development near Nordegg

## Zone 2 — Drumheller Valley Communities Beyond the Tyrrell Museum

**TDZ:** Canadian Badlands
**Corridor:** Red Deer River East (Zone 5)
**Gap rating:** High

### Why It Is Under-Served

The Royal Tyrrell Museum draws more than 450,000 visitors per year — one of Canada's largest museum attendance figures. But the communities of East Coulee, Morrin, Rosedale, and Nacmine — all within the same Red Deer River valley — capture almost no incremental visitor spend. Visitors arrive at the museum, potentially drive the valley loop road, and return to Calgary.

- East Coulee's Atlas Coal Mine National Historic Site is one of the most authentic industrial heritage sites in western Canada but receives a fraction of Tyrrell's visitor numbers.
- Rosebud Theatre, a professional dinner theatre in the heritage village of Rosebud, operates largely independently of the badlands tourism circuit.
- The valley scenic loop road (Hwy 10) passes all these sites but has no interpretive wayfinding connecting them into a circuit.

### Gap Criteria Met

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Asset-access mismatch | Partial | Road access exists but no wayfinding or circuit packaging |
| Spend concentration bypass | Yes | Visitor spend concentrates at Tyrrell; valley communities capture minimal spend |
| Accommodation floor | Yes | Accommodation is concentrated in Drumheller town; East Coulee and Rosedale have minimal overnight options |
| Marketing invisibility | Yes | Atlas Coal Mine, Rosebud Theatre, and East Coulee are not featured in a packaged badlands circuit |

### Recommended Interventions

- Develop a badlands valley circuit with interpretive wayfinding on Hwy 10 connecting Tyrrell, Horseshoe Canyon, Hoodoos, Atlas Coal Mine, and East Coulee
- Create joint ticketing between Royal Tyrrell Museum and Atlas Coal Mine NHS
- Develop a Rosebud Theatre + overnight accommodation package for the Calgary day-trip market
- Pilot a shuttle loop from Drumheller to East Coulee and Rosebud on summer weekends

## Zone 3 — Highway 2A Heritage Corridor (Olds to Calgary North)

**TDZ:** Olds to Lacombe (partial); Foothills (partial)
**Corridor:** Central Alberta into Foothills (Zones 2 and 4)
**Gap rating:** High

### Why It Is Under-Served

Highway 2A is the original Queen Elizabeth II Highway — the historic route through the small towns of Didsbury, Carstairs, Olds, Innisfail, and Lacombe between Calgary and Red Deer. Since the QEII was twinned as a divided freeway, Hwy 2A has become a quiet scenic alternative. It passes through towns with heritage main streets, local museums, and farm markets — but the highway itself is not marketed as a tourism route.

- Tens of thousands of vehicles per week use QEII as a bypass, passing within 5km of these communities without ever stopping.
- There is no corridor branding, no interpretive signage on Hwy 2A, and no packaging that positions the route as an alternative to the freeway.
- Individual communities have their own local events and markets but are not connected into a corridor product.

### Gap Criteria Met

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Asset-access mismatch | Yes | Communities are accessible but invisible to QEII bypass traffic |
| Spend concentration bypass | Yes | Drive-through traffic on QEII generates no stops or spend in corridor communities |
| Accommodation floor | Partial | Adequate accommodation in Olds and Innisfail but not in smaller communities |
| Marketing invisibility | Yes | No Hwy 2A heritage corridor branding exists in any TDZ marketing material |

### Recommended Interventions

- Develop a Hwy 2A heritage corridor brand — "Historic Heartland Route" or equivalent — with signage at both QEII on-ramps directing traffic to the scenic alternative
- Create a self-guided driving itinerary connecting heritage main streets, farm markets, and local attractions
- Package a one-night Hwy 2A loop from Calgary as an alternative to the Banff/Jasper circuit for the domestic short-break market

## Zone 4 — High River, Black Diamond, and Turner Valley Triangle

**TDZ:** Foothills
**Corridor:** Foothills and Bow South (Zone 4)
**Gap rating:** Moderate-High

### Why It Is Under-Served

The triangle formed by High River, Black Diamond, Turner Valley, and Longview sits at the western edge of the Foothills TDZ within 60-90km of Calgary. It contains Bar U Ranch NHS, Sheep River Provincial Park, Highwood River fishing, Turner Valley oil heritage, the Leighton Art Centre, and some of Alberta's most accessible foothill scenery. Yet the area is underrepresented in Foothills TDZ marketing relative to its asset quality.

- No transit from Calgary despite proximity.
- The US cross-border visitor traveling north on Hwy 2 from Montana passes through this region but has no reason to stop given the absence of wayfinding or programming.
- The oil heritage story of Turner Valley (Alberta's first oil discovery, 1914) is not integrated into a coherent cultural circuit.

### Gap Criteria Met

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Asset-access mismatch | Yes | No transit from Calgary; US cross-border traffic passes without stopping |
| Spend concentration bypass | Partial | Visitors do stop at Bar U Ranch but do not continue to adjacent communities |
| Accommodation floor | Low | Adequate accommodation exists in High River |
| Marketing invisibility | Yes | Turner Valley oil heritage and Leighton Art Centre are not featured in provincial tourism campaigns |

### Recommended Interventions

- Develop a foothills cultural circuit connecting Bar U Ranch, Turner Valley Heritage Site, Leighton Art Centre, and Longview steakhouse culture
- Target US cross-border visitors arriving via Hwy 2 with interpretive wayfinding at the border crossing
- Create a Trans-Canada Foothills loop from Calgary for the international visitor: Calgary → Okotoks → Black Diamond → Turner Valley → Nanton → return

## Zone 5 — Sundre and Upper Red Deer River Headwaters

**TDZ:** Cochrane to Sundre
**Corridor:** Bow Corridor West (Zone 1)
**Gap rating:** Moderate-High

### Why It Is Under-Served

Sundre sits at the edge of the foothills at the confluence of the Red Deer River headwaters system. It offers fishing (Raven River is Alberta's most famous brown trout fishery), camping, rafting, hiking, and equestrian access. The Sundre Rodeo is a genuine Alberta cultural event. Yet the community has almost no tourism infrastructure beyond a basic campground and rodeo grounds.

- No trailhead shuttle system despite significant summer demand for backcountry access.
- No wayfinding on Hwy 22 north of Cochrane directing visitors to Sundre and its assets.
- The Raven River fishing corridor is well known to anglers but not marketed to the broader outdoor recreation visitor.

### Gap Criteria Met

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Asset-access mismatch | Yes | Assets exist but no trailhead shuttle and limited wayfinding from Hwy 22 |
| Spend concentration bypass | Yes | Visitors pass through on Hwy 22 without stopping |
| Accommodation floor | Yes | Very limited commercial accommodation beyond campgrounds |
| Marketing invisibility | Yes | Raven River fishing, Sundre Rodeo, and upper headwaters are not featured in Cochrane to Sundre TDZ campaigns |

### Recommended Interventions

- Install wayfinding on Hwy 22 directing visitors to Sundre at both Cochrane and Olds junctions
- Develop a Sundre outdoor adventure package: Raven River fishing + Sundre Rodeo + foothills camping circuit
- Create a shuttle from Sundre to upper headwaters trailheads to reduce vehicle pressure on backcountry roads
- Partner with Olds College agri-tourism network to create a combined agriculture and outdoor recreation circuit

## Implications for Project Deliverables

- **Hub recommendations:** The Abraham Lake/Nordegg zone should be elevated within the Rocky Mountain House hub profile to reflect its critical gap-to-potential ratio.
- **Transportation plan:** Shuttle services for Zones 1 and 3 (David Thompson backcountry; Drumheller valley loop) are the highest-priority road-based interventions alongside the Sylvan Lake rail opportunity.
- **Marketing strategy:** The Hwy 2A heritage corridor and foothills cultural circuit are the clearest opportunities for a new consumer-facing corridor product.
- **RAG corpus:** This document should be indexed so stakeholders can query which zones are under-served and why.
