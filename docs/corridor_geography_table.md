# Corridor Geography Table

Date: 2026-07-07

## Purpose

This document maps the corridor geography for the Bow and Red Deer River project area. Each zone lists the anchor communities, key highways, river access points, parks and protected areas, airports and transit nodes, active rail infrastructure, and TDZ membership. This table is the primary geospatial reference for hub planning, transportation gap analysis, and under-served zone identification.

---

## Zone 1 — Bow Corridor West: Cochrane to Sundre Foothills

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Anchor Communities** | Cochrane, Sundre, Cremona, Water Valley, Carstairs |
| **Key Highways** | Hwy 1A (Bow Valley scenic route); Hwy 22 (north-south foothills spine); Hwy 27 (Sundre link east to Red Deer); Hwy 567 and 734 (backcountry and gravel access) |
| **River Access** | Bow River (Cochrane reach); Ghost River confluence; Red Deer River headwaters north of Sundre |
| **Parks and Protected Areas** | Ghost Reservoir Provincial Recreation Area; Waiparous Creek; Sundre trailhead networks; Fallen Timber Creek corridor |
| **Airports and Transit** | No commercial airport. Nearest: Calgary International (YYC), approximately 60km southeast. No scheduled transit from Calgary to Sundre. |
| **Active Rail** | No active passenger or freight rail in this zone. Former CN alignment abandoned. |
| **Tourism Assets** | Foothills hiking and camping; ghost towns (Anthracite); ranching and equestrian culture; Raven and Bearberry fishing rivers; Sundre Rodeo; Water Valley Music Festival |
| **TDZ Membership** | Cochrane to Sundre TDZ; Foothills TDZ (partial) |
| **Key Gap** | Zero transit connection from Calgary. Weekend highway bottleneck on Hwy 22 north of Cochrane. No trailhead shuttle system. |

## Zone 2 — Central Alberta: Olds to Lacombe and Red Deer Corridor

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Anchor Communities** | Olds, Innisfail, Lacombe, Ponoka, Red Deer, Sylvan Lake |
| **Key Highways** | Hwy 2 / QEII (major Calgary-Edmonton spine); Hwy 2A (historic scenic route through town centres); Hwy 11 (east-west link from Red Deer to Rocky Mountain House); Hwy 54 (north from Olds to Rocky Mountain House); Hwy 12 (east from Lacombe to Stettler) |
| **River Access** | Red Deer River (Red Deer city reach and upstream); Blindman River (Lacombe area); Medicine River (Rimbey area) |
| **Parks and Protected Areas** | Gaetz Lake Sanctuary (Red Deer); Sylvan Lake Provincial Park; Gull Lake; Dickson Dam Recreation Area; Kerry Wood Nature Centre |
| **Airports and Transit** | Red Deer Regional Airport (YQF): limited charter and general aviation, no scheduled airline service. Red Arrow intercity bus operates along Hwy 2 with stops at Red Deer and Lacombe. |
| **Active Rail** | CN mainline runs through Red Deer and Lacombe. CPKC line passes near Ponoka. Both are active freight corridors. No passenger service currently active. |
| **Tourism Assets** | Sylvan Lake beach and marina (major summer destination); Lacombe Corn Maze; Olds Agri-Food College and Open Farm Days; Central Alberta Farmers Market circuit; Red Deer Museum and Art Gallery; Heritage Ranch; Medicine River Wildlife Centre; Innisfail Aquatic Centre |
| **TDZ Membership** | Olds to Lacombe TDZ |
| **Key Gap** | No rail passenger service despite active CN and CPKC freight lines. Sylvan Lake parking congestion in summer ($50/day charge introduced) creates opportunity for rail-linked visitor access. No dedicated cycling or trail connection between corridor communities. |

## Zone 3 — West-Central: Rocky Mountain House and David Thompson Corridor

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Anchor Communities** | Rocky Mountain House, Nordegg, Caroline, Eckville, Rimbey |
| **Key Highways** | Hwy 11 (David Thompson Hwy — main east-west route from Red Deer to Nordegg and Banff National Park boundary); Hwy 22 (north-south foothills link); Hwy 54 (south from Rocky Mountain House to Olds) |
| **River Access** | North Saskatchewan River (Abraham Lake and Bighorn region); Clearwater River; Ram River; Brazeau River; Crimson Lake access |
| **Parks and Protected Areas** | Crimson Lake Provincial Park; Abraham Lake (no provincial park status but major recreation draw); Kootenay Plains Ecological Reserve; Rocky Mountain House National Historic Site; Nordegg Heritage Site |
| **Airports and Transit** | No commercial airport. Nearest: Red Deer Regional (YQF), approximately 80km east. No scheduled transit on Hwy 11. |
| **Active Rail** | No active rail in this zone. Former CN alignment to Rocky Mountain House long abandoned. |
| **Tourism Assets** | Abraham Lake (turquoise lake with distinctive frozen methane bubbles — strong social media draw); Rocky Mountain House NHS; Nordegg heritage coal mining site; David Thompson Resort; backcountry camping and hiking; wildlife viewing (elk, deer, bear corridor); fishing on North Saskatchewan system |
| **TDZ Membership** | David Thompson TDZ |
| **Key Gap** | Hwy 11 is the sole access corridor — single point of failure for visitor access. No transit. No last-mile connection to backcountry sites. Seasonal road conditions reduce shoulder-season viability. Very limited accommodation capacity in Nordegg area. |

## Zone 4 — Foothills and Bow South: Okotoks to Nanton

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Anchor Communities** | Okotoks, High River, Black Diamond, Turner Valley, Nanton, Claresholm, Vulcan |
| **Key Highways** | Hwy 2 (QEII south from Calgary); Hwy 2A (scenic historic route); Hwy 7 (east-west link from Okotoks to Brooks); Hwy 22 (foothills scenic route south); Hwy 533 and 520 (range road access to Kananaskis boundary) |
| **River Access** | Bow River (south of Calgary into Okotoks); Highwood River (High River town centre); Sheep River (Turner Valley and Black Diamond); Pekisko Creek |
| **Parks and Protected Areas** | Sheep River Provincial Park; Kananaskis Country (adjacent western boundary); Chain Lakes Provincial Park; Frank Lake (IBA bird area); Willow Creek Provincial Park |
| **Airports and Transit** | Calgary International Airport (YYC), approximately 40-80km north depending on community. Primary air gateway for this zone and for international visitors. Red Arrow Lethbridge route passes through High River area. |
| **Active Rail** | CPKC mainline passes through this region south of Calgary. No passenger service. |
| **Tourism Assets** | Turner Valley and Black Diamond oil heritage; Nanton Lancaster Air Museum; High River Museum; Okotoks Erratic (large glacial boulder); Heritage Ranching and Western culture; Bar U Ranch National Historic Site; Leighton Art Centre; spring and fall bird migration (Frank Lake); Vulcan Star Trek tourism |
| **TDZ Membership** | Foothills TDZ |
| **Key Gap** | Strongest economic potential TDZ but under-served by dedicated tourism transit from Calgary. Last-mile access to foothills recreation areas requires personal vehicle. Cross-border US visitors pass through this zone on Hwy 2 but minimal stopping infrastructure exists. |

## Zone 5 — Red Deer River East: Drumheller and Canadian Badlands

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Anchor Communities** | Drumheller, Hanna, Morrin, Rosedale, East Coulee, Carbon, Beiseker |
| **Key Highways** | Hwy 9 (main route east from Calgary to Drumheller, 138km); Hwy 56 (north-south through Red Deer River valley); Hwy 27 (west link to Three Hills and Red Deer); Hwy 36 (south link to Hanna and prairie interior); Hwy 10 (Drumheller valley scenic loop road) |
| **River Access** | Red Deer River (runs through Drumheller valley and badlands canyon system); Rosebud River |
| **Parks and Protected Areas** | Midland Provincial Park (Drumheller); Horseshoe Canyon; Dry Island Buffalo Jump Provincial Park; Dinosaur Provincial Park (further east, UNESCO World Heritage Site); Bleriot Ferry crossing |
| **Airports and Transit** | No commercial airport in Drumheller. Nearest: Calgary International (YYC), approximately 138km west via Hwy 9. No scheduled transit service. |
| **Active Rail** | No active rail serving Drumheller. Tracks no longer in place. Former rail lines to Drumheller long removed. |
| **Tourism Assets** | Royal Tyrrell Museum of Palaeontology (world-class, 450,000+ annual visitors); Hoodoos trail; Horseshoe Canyon viewpoint; East Coulee School Museum; Atlas Coal Mine National Historic Site; Drumheller valley scenic drive (World's Largest Dinosaur statue); Rosebud Theatre; dark sky viewing |
| **TDZ Membership** | Canadian Badlands TDZ |
| **Key Gap** | No transit from Calgary — all 450,000+ annual visitors arrive by personal vehicle. No rail (tracks removed). Seasonal access limitations. Accommodation capacity significantly below peak summer demand. Highway 9 is the sole practical access route. |

## Cross-Zone Infrastructure Summary

| Zone | Active Rail | Commercial Airport | Scheduled Transit | Highway Spine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bow Corridor West | None | None (YYC 60km) | None | Hwy 22, Hwy 1A |
| Central Alberta | CN + CPKC (freight) | YQF (limited) | Red Arrow (partial) | Hwy 2 / QEII |
| West-Central (RMH) | None | None (YQF 80km) | None | Hwy 11 |
| Foothills / Bow South | CPKC (freight) | YYC (40-80km) | Red Arrow (partial) | Hwy 2, Hwy 22 |
| Red Deer River East | None | None (YYC 138km) | None | Hwy 9 |

**Key finding:** Central Alberta is the only corridor zone with active rail infrastructure (CN and CPKC freight lines through Red Deer and Lacombe). This makes it the strongest candidate for a rail-linked tourism access intervention, particularly for the Sylvan Lake summer congestion problem identified by the project stakeholder.
