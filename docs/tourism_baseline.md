# Alberta Tourism Baseline Profile

Date: 2026-07-03

## Purpose

This document compiles a baseline tourism profile for Alberta and Central Alberta using publicly available Travel Alberta indicator data. It provides the quantitative foundation for the economic impact analysis in the final report and supplies the RAG corpus with provincial-level context so stakeholders can compare corridor-level findings against broader Alberta trends.

---

## 1. Economic Impact

- Tourism is one of Alberta's major economic sectors, generating direct and indirect contributions to provincial GDP.
- Travel Alberta tracks economic impact through visitor spending, employment, and tax revenue indicators.
- The provincial economy benefits from both domestic and international visitor markets, with domestic travel accounting for the majority of overnight trips.
- Rural and corridor communities benefit unevenly from tourism activity. The majority of visitor spend is concentrated in Calgary, Edmonton, and Banff/Jasper, leaving Central Alberta communities under-served relative to their asset base.

## 2. Employment

- Tourism employment is tracked by Travel Alberta and Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey.
- Tourism supports jobs in accommodation, food and beverage, recreation and entertainment, transportation, and retail sectors.
- The five priority TDZs for this project have a combined estimated potential of 8,351 new jobs over a 10-year horizon:
  - David Thompson: 1,838 potential jobs
  - Olds to Lacombe: 719 potential jobs
  - Cochrane to Sundre: 1,073 potential jobs
  - Foothills: 3,381 potential jobs
  - Canadian Badlands: 1,340 potential jobs
- Employment is highly seasonal in rural Alberta, with most tourism jobs concentrated in June through August.

## 3. Visitor Spend

- Travel Alberta tracks visitor spend by origin market (domestic, US, overseas) and by category (accommodation, food, transportation, recreation, retail).
- The five priority TDZs have a combined estimated tourism spend growth potential of $1,151M over a 10-year horizon:
  - David Thompson: $252M estimated tourism spend growth (7.2% annual visitor spend growth)
  - Olds to Lacombe: $98M estimated tourism spend growth (3.9% annual visitor spend growth)
  - Cochrane to Sundre: $147M estimated tourism spend growth (3.8% annual visitor spend growth)
  - Foothills: $468M estimated tourism spend growth (4.5% annual visitor spend growth)
  - Canadian Badlands: $186M estimated tourism spend growth (4.5% annual visitor spend growth)
- Visitor spend in rural zones is lower per trip than in mountain and urban destinations, partly because accommodation and activity options are more limited.

## 4. Visitation Trends

- Alberta sees strong domestic visitation from within-province travelers, particularly from Calgary and Edmonton.
- US visitors are the largest international market, typically arriving by road through southern border crossings.
- International (non-US) visitors concentrate heavily in Banff and Jasper and tend not to disperse deeply into rural Central Alberta.
- Visitation to Central Alberta is largely drive-through traffic that does not stop overnight, representing an unmet dispersal opportunity.
- Post-pandemic recovery has shown strong demand for outdoor recreation, nature-based tourism, and rural travel across Canada, trends that benefit the corridor project's hub concepts.

## 5. Seasonality

- Alberta tourism is highly seasonal. The peak season runs June through August with a secondary peak around the winter holiday period for urban and mountain destinations.
- Rural Central Alberta sees sharper summer concentration than Calgary or Banff because it lacks strong winter or shoulder-season products.
- Shoulder-season gaps (April–May and September–October) represent the largest unrealized opportunity for corridor hubs.
- Activities that extend the shoulder season include fall foliage touring, harvest festivals, agritourism, dark sky viewing, and river valley hiking.
- The hub screening framework includes seasonality potential as a criterion specifically to address this compression.

## 6. Hotel and Accommodation Performance

- Travel Alberta tracks hotel occupancy, average daily rate, and revenue per available room (RevPAR) across Alberta markets.
- Urban markets (Calgary, Edmonton) consistently show higher occupancy than rural Central Alberta.
- Rural accommodation capacity along the corridor is limited, which constrains overnight visitor spend even when attraction demand exists.
- Campgrounds and RV parks serve as the primary accommodation type in rural corridor communities. Their occupancy and reservation data are not fully centralized but provide a proxy for summer demand pressure.
- Expanding accommodation capacity or connecting existing accommodations to marketing networks is a recurring theme in TDZ stakeholder feedback.

## 7. Air Access

- Calgary International Airport (YYC) is the primary air gateway for the project corridor.
- YYC serves direct international routes from the US, UK, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, making it the main entry point for international visitors who might extend into corridor territory.
- Red Deer Regional Airport (YQF) handles limited charter and general aviation activity and is not a major international gateway.
- Air access to the corridor itself is limited. Most visitor air arrivals rent vehicles or join tours from Calgary or Edmonton.
- Improving ground transportation links between YYC and corridor destinations is more actionable than building new air capacity.

## 8. Market Size and Origin

- Alberta's domestic tourism market is anchored by Calgary (population approximately 1.4 million) and Edmonton (population approximately 1.1 million).
- Calgary is the primary source market for the Bow corridor, Foothills, Cochrane-Sundre, and Drumheller hubs.
- Edmonton is a secondary source market for the David Thompson and Olds-Lacombe hubs.
- The US drive market via Interstate 15 and Highway 2 is significant for the Foothills and southern Bow corridor hubs.
- International travelers are a growth opportunity for Drumheller (Royal Tyrrell Museum is a recognized global paleontology destination) and for Rocky Mountain House (David Thompson corridor).

## 9. Key Baseline Gaps

The following data is needed but not yet collected:

| Data Item | Why Needed | Source to Target |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal visitor counts by month for Central Alberta | Quantify seasonality compression | Statistics Canada Travel Survey of Residents |
| Accommodation occupancy rates by community | Measure capacity and demand match | Travel Alberta hotel performance data |
| Origin-destination data for Hwy 2 and Hwy 11 travellers | Estimate pass-through vs. stopping visitor share | Alberta Transportation traffic counts |
| Overnight visitor proportion for each hub area | Distinguish day-trip from overnight tourism | Visitor exit survey data |
| Indigenous tourism operator inventory | Identify partners and capacity | Indigenous Tourism Alberta |
| Royal Tyrrell Museum annual visitor count | Anchor Drumheller hub demand estimate | Royal Tyrrell Museum annual report |
