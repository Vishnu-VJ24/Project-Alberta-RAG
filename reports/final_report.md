This report documents three weeks of research and analytical work on the Central Alberta Tourism Corridor Strategy project, focused on the Bow and Red Deer River corridors in Central Alberta. The project is building a comprehensive tourism development strategy to be presented at the Integrated Transportation and Economic Corridor Summit in September 2026.

The internship produced twelve research and analytical documents, a working AI-powered research assistant deployed publicly on the web, and a structured analytical foundation covering TDZ alignment, candidate hub identification and scoring, tourism baseline indicators, geospatial asset mapping, infrastructure gap analysis, and under-served zone identification.

- Five candidate tourism hubs were identified and scored across the Bow and Red Deer River corridors. - The David Thompson zone — Abraham Lake and Nordegg backcountry — has the highest gap-to-potential ratio of any zone in the project area and represents the strongest case for targeted infrastructure investment.

## 1. Project Context and Scope

### 1.1 Project Overview

The Central Alberta Tourism Corridor Strategy project is developing a comprehensive strategic plan for tourism development along the Bow and Red Deer River corridors. The strategy addresses tourism trends, demographic change, under-serviced tourism zones, transportation needs, recreation adventure hubs, economic impact, and marketing and branding strategy.

The primary deliverables are:
- A comprehensive tourism development report aligned with Alberta's Tourism Development Zone framework

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Research Approach

The project uses a mixed-methods approach combining:
- **Desk research:** Travel Alberta TDZ reports, Statistics Canada travel data, federal and provincial policy frameworks, Parks Canada documentation, and operator information
- **Stakeholder engagement:** Direct input from the project partner on transportation infrastructure realities
- **Structured analysis:** Multi-criteria scoring frameworks applied consistently across zones and hubs
- **RAG corpus:** All research is indexed into a searchable knowledge base, making findings queryable by stakeholders

### 2.2 Hub Screening Framework

Candidate hubs were scored on eight criteria, each rated 0–5:

| Criterion | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| TDZ alignment | Strength of fit with the TDZ's strategic priorities |
| Economic potential | Estimated visitor spend growth and job creation potential |
| Corridor access | Quality and redundancy of highway and transit access |
| Accommodation capacity | Ability to convert day visitors to overnight guests |
| Tourism asset density | Number and quality of attractions within the hub area |
| Seasonality potential | Ability to generate visits in shoulder and off-peak seasons |
| Stakeholder readiness | Evidence of local support for tourism development |
| RAG evidence strength | Quality and completeness of available source material |

### 2.3 Asset Gap Framework

Tourism zones were assessed against four infrastructure categories to identify gaps:
1. **Access infrastructure** — highway quality, transit, last-mile connections, wayfinding
2. **Accommodation capacity** — overnight options relative to peak demand
3. **Visitor services** — interpretation, food, rest stops, information centres
4. **Marketing connectivity** — inclusion in TDZ campaigns, booking platforms, itineraries

### 2.4 Under-Served Zone Criteria

A zone qualifies as under-served if it meets at least two of:
1. **Asset-access mismatch** — assets exist but cannot be easily reached
2. **Spend concentration bypass** — visitors pass through or stop only at anchor, not surrounding communities
3. **Accommodation floor** — insufficient overnight capacity to convert day visits
4. **Marketing invisibility** — not included in any consumer-facing corridor itinerary

## 3. Tourism Development Zone Analysis (Week 1)

### 3.1 TDZ Profiles Summary

**David Thompson TDZ**
Covers the Rocky Mountain House and Nordegg corridor along Highway 11. Highest annual visitor spend growth rate (7.2%) and second-highest estimated job creation (1,838). Contains Abraham Lake — internationally recognized for frozen methane bubble photography — and the Nordegg heritage coal mining site. Primary weakness: sole highway access (Hwy 11), no transit, very limited backcountry accommodation. Resident support for increased visitation is strong.

**Olds to Lacombe TDZ**
Central Alberta agricultural corridor between Calgary and Red Deer. Lowest spend growth and job potential of the five TDZs but uniquely positioned for agritourism and rural experience products. Home to Sylvan Lake — the strongest near-term transportation intervention site. Active CN and CPKC freight rail lines create a viable passenger rail activation opportunity. Olds College and the agricultural fair circuit provide a distinctive identity anchor.

**Cochrane to Sundre TDZ**
Foothills and mountain gateway corridor north of Calgary. 3.8% annual visitor spend growth. Strong outdoor recreation assets including the Raven River (Alberta's premier brown trout fishery), Sundre Rodeo, Water Valley Music Festival, and Ghost Reservoir. No transit from Calgary. Hwy 22 weekend bottleneck is a persistent access barrier.

**Foothills TDZ**
Highest absolute economic potential of the five TDZs — $468M in estimated ten-year spend growth and 3,381 potential jobs. Closest zone to Calgary's 1.4 million-person source market. Contains Bar U Ranch NHS, Nanton Lancaster Air Museum, Highwood River, and Turner Valley oil heritage. US cross-border visitor market via Hwy 2 is underexploited. Nearest to deployment-ready of all five zones.

**Canadian Badlands TDZ**
Home to the Royal Tyrrell Museum of Palaeontology — one of Canada's most visited museums with over 450,000 annual visitors. $186M in estimated ten-year spend growth. Critical infrastructure gap: all visitors arrive by personal vehicle on Hwy 9, rail tracks no longer exist, no transit from Calgary. Visitor spend is concentrated at the museum and does not disperse into valley communities (East Coulee, Rosedale, Morrin).

### 3.2 Hub Screening Framework Development

Week 1 established the eight-criteria scoring framework used throughout the project. The framework was designed to:
- Produce scores comparable across very different geographic and economic contexts
- Capture both quantitative (TDZ spend figures, job estimates) and qualitative (stakeholder readiness, RAG evidence) dimensions
- Create a defensible prioritization basis for the final report recommendations

## 5. RAG Research Assistant System (Week 2)

### 5.1 System Architecture

The RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) system enables stakeholders to query the project's research corpus using natural language and receive cited answers. The system runs entirely on free infrastructure.

| Component | Technology | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Document ingestion | Python + sentence-transformers | Reads markdown docs, chunks, embeds |
| Vector index | FAISS (faiss-cpu) | Stores and searches 384-dimension embeddings |
| Embedding model | all-MiniLM-L6-v2 | Converts text to semantic vectors (CPU, free) |
| LLM | Groq API — Llama 3.3 70B Versatile | Generates cited answers (free tier) |
| Application | Streamlit | Web UI for stakeholder queries |
| Hosting | Streamlit Community Cloud | Free public hosting, auto-redeploys on GitHub push |

### 5.2 Live Application

**URL:** https://project-alberta-rag-wkw3tteb56qorgngped57g.streamlit.app/

The application is publicly accessible with no login required. It auto-redeploys every time a new commit is pushed to the GitHub main branch.

**Current corpus (as of July 11, 2026):** 131 chunks across 20 project documents.

### 5.3 How to Add New Documents

To expand the RAG corpus with new research:

1. Add new markdown files (`.md`) to the `docs/` or `reports/` directories
2. Run `python src/ingest.py` from the project root — this rebuilds the FAISS index
3. Commit and push `data/vector_store/index.faiss` and `data/vector_store/metadata.json` to GitHub
4. Streamlit Cloud auto-redeploys within 2 minutes — the new documents are live

### 5.4 API Key Management

The Groq API key is stored in Streamlit Community Cloud secrets (not in the GitHub repository). To update it:
1. Go to share.streamlit.io
2. Find the deployed app → three-dot menu → Settings → Secrets
3. Update `GROQ_API_KEY = "gsk_..."`

### 5.5 RAG Evaluation Framework

A 34-question evaluation test set is documented in `docs/rag_evaluation_questions.md`. The questions cover five categories:
- TDZ alignment and economic potential (8 questions)
- Candidate hub recommendations (8 questions)
- Transportation and corridor access (6 questions)
- Tourism baseline and seasonality (6 questions)
- RAG system scope (6 questions)

Minimum acceptable performance targets:
- 90% of answers include at least one relevant citation
- 85% of answers avoid unsupported claims
- 80% of answers are useful without manual rewriting

### 5.6 Known Limitations

- The corpus is currently built from internal project documents only. External sources (Travel Alberta TDZ reports as text, Statistics Canada data) have not yet been scraped and indexed. This is Week 4's planned corpus expansion.
- With a small corpus, the model can occasionally attribute figures from one TDZ to another when multiple similar numbers appear in adjacent chunks. Adding dedicated per-TDZ source documents would significantly reduce this.
- The system performs best on questions that are directly addressed in the indexed documents. It correctly flags uncertainty when evidence is absent.

## 6. Tourism Baseline Profile (Week 2)

### 6.1 Key Indicators

**Economic impact:** Alberta tourism is a major provincial economic sector. Central Alberta sits between the two highest-volume corridors — Calgary-Banff and Edmonton — but captures a disproportionately small share of visitor spending relative to its asset base.

**Employment:** Tourism employment is highly seasonal in rural Alberta. Most rural jobs are concentrated in June through August. The combined potential across the five TDZs is 8,351 jobs over a ten-year horizon.

**Visitor spend:** The combined ten-year tourism spend growth potential across the five TDZs is $1.15 billion. The Foothills TDZ alone accounts for $468M — 41% of the total.

**Seasonality:** Shoulder season compression is a fundamental structural problem for the corridor. Rural Central Alberta is almost entirely summer-dependent. Fall foliage, harvest festivals, agritourism, dark sky viewing, and river valley hiking are the strongest candidates for extending the season.

**Source markets:** Calgary (1.4M) is the primary source market for four of five hubs. Edmonton (1.1M) is secondary for the David Thompson and Olds-Lacombe hubs. The US drive market via Hwy 2 from Montana is an underexploited segment for the Foothills and Drumheller hubs. The Royal Tyrrell Museum is the strongest asset for attracting international visitors.

**Air access:** Calgary International (YYC) is the sole major gateway. Visitors arriving by air rent vehicles or join tours. Ground transportation links between YYC and corridor destinations are more actionable than new air capacity.

### 7.2 Sylvan Lake Rail Transportation Opportunity

Based on stakeholder input from July 6, 2026:

**The opportunity:** Sylvan Lake charges $50/day for summer parking — a direct market signal of excess demand. A rail shuttle from Red Deer County at $10 one way using the CN right-of-way would save visitors $30 per trip versus driving and parking.

**The infrastructure:** CN mainline and CPKC lines both run through the Red Deer / Lacombe County area. No new track is required.

**Economic model:**

| Scenario | Daily Visitors | Conversion Rate | Daily Rail Riders | Daily Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 5,000 | 10% | 500 | $10,000 |

## 8. Asset Gap Analysis (Week 3)

### 8.1 Four Structural Gaps — Corridor-Wide

**1. Transit Desert**
Every corridor zone operates as a complete transit desert. All visitors arrive by personal vehicle. This limits visitor reach, creates peak-day highway congestion, and excludes the growing urban visitor segment that prefers not to drive.

**2. Visitor Spend Concentration**
In every zone, visitor spend concentrates at one or two anchor assets. The Tyrrell Museum, Sylvan Lake, and Abraham Lake each capture the majority of their zone's visitor attention. Surrounding communities receive minimal incremental benefit.

**3. Accommodation Ceiling**
In three of five zones, accommodation capacity is the binding constraint on overnight visitor conversion. Day trips dominate not by preference but by necessity — visitors who would stay overnight cannot find suitable options.

**4. Marketing Disconnection**
Assets in each zone are not connected into bookable, marketed circuits. TDZ planning documents exist but have not translated into consumer-facing itinerary products.

### 8.2 Zone Gap Ratings

| Zone | Gap Rating | Primary Gap |
|---|---|---|
| David Thompson (Rocky Mountain House) | **Critical** | Access, accommodation, last-mile all absent |
| Canadian Badlands (Drumheller) | **Critical** | No transit, accommodation below demand, spend concentration |
| Cochrane to Sundre | **High** | No transit, limited accommodation, wayfinding |
| Foothills | **High** | No Calgary transit link despite proximity, US market underexploited |
| Olds to Lacombe / Central Alberta | **Moderate** | Rail opportunity untapped, agritourism packaging missing |

### 8.3 Priority Investments by Gap Type

| Priority | Gap Type | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Critical | Transit and access | Sylvan Lake rail shuttle; David Thompson shuttle; Drumheller road improvements |
| 2 — High | Accommodation capacity | Glamping / cabin development at Nordegg and Drumheller |
| 3 — High | Visitor spend dispersal | Cross-asset circuit packaging with joint ticketing |
| 4 — Moderate | Last-mile connections | Abraham Lake visitor node; Drumheller valley loop wayfinding |
| 5 — Moderate | Marketing connectivity | Foothills cultural circuit; badlands valley loop; Hwy 2A heritage route |

## 9. Under-Served Zone Analysis (Week 3)

### 9.1 Five Priority Under-Served Zones

**Priority 1 — Abraham Lake and Nordegg Backcountry** (David Thompson TDZ)
World-class scenic and heritage assets. No transit, no staffed visitor facility at Abraham Lake, almost no commercial accommodation. Highest gap-to-potential ratio in the project. Recommended interventions: basic visitor node at Abraham Lake; seasonal shuttle from Rocky Mountain House; package the David Thompson Hwy as a 2-day bookable circuit.

**Priority 2 — Drumheller Valley Communities** (Canadian Badlands TDZ)
East Coulee, Morrin, Rosedale and Rosebud are completely bypassed by the 450,000+ visitors going to the Royal Tyrrell Museum. Atlas Coal Mine NHS and Rosebud Theatre are excellent but disconnected assets. Recommended interventions: valley loop wayfinding on Hwy 10; joint ticketing with Tyrrell; Rosebud overnight package; summer weekend shuttle loop.

**Priority 3 — Highway 2A Heritage Corridor** (Olds to Lacombe / Foothills TDZs)
The original QEII highway through Olds, Innisfail, Lacombe, and Didsbury has heritage main streets, farm markets, and agricultural attractions. Tens of thousands of vehicles per week pass within 5km on the freeway without stopping. No corridor brand or wayfinding exists. Recommended interventions: "Historic Heartland Route" branding; directional signage at QEII on-ramps; self-guided driving itinerary.

**Priority 4 — High River, Black Diamond, and Turner Valley Triangle** (Foothills TDZ)
Bar U Ranch NHS, Sheep River Provincial Park, Turner Valley oil heritage, and Leighton Art Centre within 80km of Calgary. No Calgary transit link. US cross-border traffic on Hwy 2 has no stopping infrastructure. Recommended interventions: foothills cultural circuit with joint programming; US cross-border wayfinding at Hwy 2 entry; Trans-Canada Foothills loop itinerary.

**Priority 5 — Sundre and Upper Red Deer Headwaters** (Cochrane to Sundre TDZ)
Raven River (Alberta's most celebrated brown trout fishery), Sundre Rodeo, foothills camping. No trailhead shuttle, no wayfinding on Hwy 22. Limited commercial accommodation. Recommended interventions: Hwy 22 wayfinding from Cochrane and Olds junctions; Sundre outdoor adventure package; upper headwaters trailhead shuttle.

## 10. Key Strategic Recommendations

### 10.1 Transportation

**Immediate priority — Sylvan Lake CN rail shuttle:**
Activate the existing CN right-of-way between Red Deer County and Sylvan Lake for a seasonal tourist shuttle at $10 one way. The $50/day parking charge is a real demand signal. No new track required. Present as the centrepiece transportation recommendation at the September summit.

**Near-term — David Thompson backcountry shuttle:**
Develop a seasonal shuttle from Rocky Mountain House to Abraham Lake and Nordegg. Currently all backcountry visitors must have a private vehicle. A shuttle would open the zone's world-class scenery to a broader visitor segment and reduce highway pressure.

**Near-term — Drumheller valley loop wayfinding and shuttle:**
Install interpretive wayfinding on Hwy 10 connecting Tyrrell, Horseshoe Canyon, Atlas Coal Mine, and East Coulee. Pilot a summer weekend shuttle. The 450,000 Tyrrell visitors are the most underutilized dispersal opportunity in the project area.

**Medium-term — Hwy 2A heritage route:**
Brand Hwy 2A as a heritage corridor alternative to the QEII with directional signage at both on-ramps. Lowest cost of any transportation recommendation; highest potential reach given existing freeway traffic volume.

### 10.2 Hub Development

**Foothills hub:** Prioritize first. Strongest economic potential, closest to Calgary, nearest to deployment-ready. Focus on Calgary transit link and US cross-border visitor capture.

**Drumheller hub:** Focus investment on visitor dispersal from the Tyrrell into the valley, not on anchor development — the anchor already works. Accommodation expansion and valley circuit packaging are the interventions.

**David Thompson hub:** Make the investment case for the Abraham Lake visitor node and seasonal shuttle. The gap-to-potential story is the strongest in the project — it should be a centrepiece of the final report.

**Central Alberta hub:** Advance the Sylvan Lake rail proposal through CN/CPKC engagement. Collect real visitor count data from Lacombe County before the summit.

**Cochrane-Sundre hub:** Develop trailhead shuttle and wayfinding. Package Raven River fishing and Sundre Rodeo as a combined outdoor-culture product.

### 10.3 Marketing and Branding

- Develop the Highway 2A "Historic Heartland Route" as a consumer-facing corridor product — distinct from the mountain and badlands circuits
- Create a foothills cultural circuit connecting Bar U Ranch, Turner Valley, Leighton Art Centre, and Nanton with joint programming and shared ticketing
- Target the US cross-border visitor explicitly on Hwy 2 between the Montana border and Calgary
- Develop a badlands valley loop brand connecting Tyrrell, Atlas Coal Mine, Hoodoos, and Rosebud Theatre
